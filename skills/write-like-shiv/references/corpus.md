Notes describe inspected passages or clearly identified catalog information. Recommendations and craft interpretations are editorial judgments.

Preference labels: **Exact** means Shiv named the work; **Author** means Shiv named the author and this work was selected for study; **Publisher** means Shiv named the publication family; **Collection** means Shiv named the d.school collection; **Suggested** means a new recommendation. ## Core influences

### C01 · Paul Graham · Why Smart People Have Bad Ideas

[Original essay](https://paulgraham.com/bronze.html) · [User's YC link](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/91-why-smart-people-have-bad-ideas). Exact. Essay, April 2005. Original text sampled; YC page did not expose body text.

**Craft:** Begins with an observed classification problem, implicates the writer through Artix, then uses a painting analogy to explain attachment to a weak idea. The generalization follows the failure. **Transfer:** In an engineering or business essay, explain the mistake before naming the pattern. **Limit:** The author's experience is evidence about his experience, not permission to invent Shiv's.

### C02 · Paul Graham · Default Alive or Default Dead?

[Original essay](https://paulgraham.com/aord.html) · [User's YC link](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/95-default-alive-or-default-dead). Exact. Essay, October 2015. Original text sampled.

**Craft:** Opens with a diagnostic whose assumptions are stated, then lets the answer determine the next conversation. It separates the current trajectory from hope about intervention. **Transfer:** Build a decision memo around a test the reader can actually apply. **Limit:** Preserve the test's assumptions; its force depends on them.

### C03 · Paul Graham · Relentlessly Resourceful

[Original essay](https://paulgraham.com/relres.html) · [User's YC link](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/94-be-relentlessly-resourceful). Exact. Essay, March 2009. Short essay text inspected.

**Craft:** Searches for a precise name by rejecting adjacent meanings and an imperfect metaphor. Brief sentences punctuate that reasoning. **Transfer:** Define a quality through behavior and boundaries before turning it into advice. **Limit:** The essay itself distinguishes startup obstacles from the problems of artistic discovery; do not universalize its slogan.

### C04 · Paul Graham · A Student's Guide to Startups

[Original essay](https://paulgraham.com/mit.html) · [User's YC link](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/93-a-student-s-guide-to-startups). Exact. Essay derived from a talk, October 2006. Original text sampled.

**Craft:** Anticipates an ambitious reader's next objection and revises an earlier position in public. The explanation makes social incentives concrete. **Transfer:** Let the reader see which observation changed the recommendation. **Limit:** Keep the historical startup advice separate from current advice and from the craft being studied.

### C05 · Paul Graham · How to Do Great Work

[Essay](https://paulgraham.com/greatwork.html). Exact. July 2023. Substantial text sampled.

**Craft:** Develops an extended argument through questions, qualifications, examples, and connections across fields. Its treatment of style explicitly rejects affectation. **Transfer:** Use curiosity to find a better question, then allow structure to grow with the reasoning. **Limit:** Its length and ambition are appropriate to a substantial essay; they would overwhelm a work message.

### C06 · Paul Graham · Writing, Briefly

[Essay](https://paulgraham.com/writing44.html). Author. March 2005. Short essay inspected.

**Craft:** Compresses writing advice into a deliberately flowing sequence of practical operations. Revision is part of generating thought. **Transfer:** Draft something concrete, discover what is wrong, cut, and read it aloud. **Limit:** The unusual long sequence is a device for this piece, not a mandatory sentence shape.

### C07 · Richard P. Feynman · Six Easy Pieces

Exact book. The inspected primary reading is the corresponding lecture material: [Atoms in Motion](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_01.html) and [Conservation of Energy](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_04.html), from The Feynman Lectures on Physics. These are chapter samples, not a claim to have inspected the full named book or its edition-specific front matter.

**Craft:** Acknowledges what is unknown, builds a simple physical picture, and carefully relates an analogy to an abstract quantity. **Transfer:** Give the reader a usable model and show its limits. **Limit:** Accessibility must preserve the underlying meaning; an analogy is not an independent demonstration.

### C08 · David Foster Wallace · This Is Water

[Kenyon's published text](https://bulletin-archive.kenyon.edu/x4280.html). Author. Commencement address; text sampled.

**Craft:** Moves from an ordinary story into a sustained everyday scene, while questioning the speaker's own default interpretation. Accumulation lets the reader experience the problem. **Transfer:** In reflective essays, notice the narrator's participation and retain complications that matter. **Limit:** Do not import constant digression, mimic the signature syntax, or invent a scene from Shiv's life.

### C09 · George Carlin · Brain Droppings

[Publisher record](https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/titles/george-carlin/brain-droppings/9780306835544/). Author. Humor collection. Catalog description inspected; full book text was not available for this analysis. Edition-specific catalog dates are not treated as the original publication date.

**Study question:** How do a verbal assumption, category mismatch, or euphemism generate a short observation or extended routine? The publisher confirms a mixture of short material and monologues. **Provisional transfer:** Use precise language to expose an absurdity. **Limit:** The detailed humor model still needs a passage Shiv selects; avoid importing default aggression or profanity.

### C10 · Steve Jobs · Stanford Commencement Address

[Stanford's prepared text](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2005/06/youve-got-find-love-jobs-says). Author. June 12, 2005. Text sampled.

**Craft:** Uses a small number of autobiographical stories with concrete scenes, a turning point, and retrospective meaning. **Transfer:** Let a verified experience bear a larger claim. **Limit:** A speech's repetition and emotional closure do not automatically suit an email; do not turn retrospective narrative into a universal law of success.

### C11 · Steve Jobs · Make Something Wonderful

[Official digital book](https://book.stevejobsarchive.com/). Author. Curated collection; selected emails inspected, including “Today” dated May 26, 2010, and the email to himself dated September 2, 2010. Full collection not read.

**Craft:** The two emails show different registers: an operational note returns attention to work; a private reflection accumulates concrete dependencies. **Transfer:** Change register with the audience and purpose while keeping clear nouns and a coherent thought. **Limit:** Useful repetition is not a defect merely because a detector or heuristic might notice it.

### C12 · Sam Altman · How To Be Successful

[Essay](https://blog.samaltman.com/how-to-be-successful). Author. Text sampled.

**Craft:** Packages a broad position into memorable operational claims, then qualifies confidence with self-awareness and truth-seeking. **Transfer:** Make a recommendation easy to retain and connect it to action. **Limit:** Do not reproduce the certainty of a maxim when the supplied evidence supports only a conditional claim.

### C13 · Sam Altman · Productivity

[Essay](https://blog.samaltman.com/productivity). Author. Text sampled.

**Craft:** Establishes choice of work before discussing execution, then uses first-person practices to explain the recommendation. **Transfer:** Put the upstream decision before implementation tricks. **Limit:** Separate personal practice from tested general advice; do not copy numerical claims into a new argument without checking them.

### C14 · David Airey · Logo Design Love

[Author's book catalog](https://www.davidairey.com/books). Author; name confirmed by Shiv in this conversation. Design book. Catalog inspected; the book's full prose and visuals were not inspected.

**Study question:** How does a design explanation connect the brief, visual alternatives, and final judgment? **Provisional transfer:** Explain a choice with criteria and examples rather than decorative adjectives. **Limit:** This is a selection for deeper study, not sentence-level evidence from the book. Use C15 for directly inspected Airey prose.

### C15 · David Airey · Being an independent designer

[Author's essay](https://archive.davidairey.com/dealing-with-designers/being-an-independent-designer/). Author. Text sampled.

**Craft:** Starts with the event that led to self-employment, then presents freedoms beside their practical costs: choosing prices also means bearing uncertainty about them. **Transfer:** Explain the desirable consequence and the responsibility attached to it. **Limit:** Learn the substance of paired tradeoffs without forcing every paragraph into a contrast slogan.

### C16 · Ezra Klein · How politics makes us stupid

[Original Vox article](https://www.vox.com/2014/4/6/5556462/brain-dead-how-politics-makes-us-stupid). Author. April 6, 2014. Opening and experiment explanation sampled.

**Craft:** Sets up competing explanations and makes their difference visible through an experiment. **Transfer:** Identify what evidence would distinguish two plausible mechanisms. **Limit:** Treat research discussed in a historical article as something to recheck before using it as present-day evidence.

### C17 · Lee Kuan Yew · Opening of Trade Union House and Singapore Conference Hall

[National Archives of Singapore transcript](https://www.nas.gov.sg/archivesonline/data/pdfdoc/lky19651015.pdf). Author. Speech, October 15, 1965. Transcript sections sampled.

**Craft:** Organizes persuasion around constraints, choices, and consequences, repeatedly returning to the central practical stake. **Transfer:** Explain what an institution must make possible and what tradeoff a policy imposes. **Limit:** This is historical political rhetoric. Studying it establishes neither agreement with its politics nor evidence for its causal claims; avoid importing its coercive tone into ordinary writing.

### C18 · Cal Newport · Deep Habits: The Importance of Planning Every Minute of Your Work Day

[Author's article](https://calnewport.com/deep-habits-the-importance-of-planning-every-minute-of-your-work-day/). Author. Main article text inspected; reader comments excluded.

**Craft:** Makes a method tangible through a notebook and a schedule, explains how it is used, then addresses practical objections. **Transfer:** Present an inspectable artifact and the adjustments that make the method usable. **Limit:** Personal productivity estimates are not universal measurements; omit the status judgment about other workers.

### C19 · Cal Newport · What Would Happen If We Slowed Down?

[Author's article](https://calnewport.com/what-would-happen-if-we-slowed-down/). Author. Main article text inspected; reader comments excluded.

**Craft:** Offers a mechanism, explores the counterfactual, and leaves a genuine uncertainty open. **Transfer:** Ask which assumed benefit has actually been tested. **Limit:** Preserve the provisional nature of the argument and do not turn its illustrative workload proportions into an empirical law.

### C20 · Seema Amble · The Incumbents Are Coming

[a16z article](https://a16z.com/the-incumbents-are-coming/). Exact. September 3, 2026. Article text sampled.

**Craft:** Starts with the serious incumbent case, distinguishes access to records from doing a job, and develops operational criteria through examples. **Transfer:** Define the unit of competition and the learning mechanism before recommending strategy. **Limit:** This is a venture investor's argument. Attribute factual claims and independently verify them when they become load-bearing; “a16z” is a publisher, not one voice.

### C21 · Ben Horowitz · Good Product Manager/Bad Product Manager

[a16z article](https://a16z.com/good-product-manager-bad-product-manager/). Publisher. Posted June 15, 2012; the article itself warns that the underlying document was already old. Text inspected.

**Craft:** Converts expectations into observable behaviors with repeated comparisons. **Transfer:** Write role standards a colleague could assess. **Limit:** The writer explicitly presents it as a historical training-document example. Neither its dated management model nor its repeated good/bad framing belongs in every new piece.

## Recommended adjacent writers

These are proposals, not claims about Shiv's existing reading. Start with R01, R05, R08, R02, and R03 for the broadest craft coverage. Fit judgments below are editorial confidence, not measured preference probabilities.

### R01 · Richard Hamming · You and Your Research

[Talk transcript](https://www.cs.virginia.edu/~robins/YouAndYourResearch.html). Suggested. March 7, 1986. Transcript sampled. **Fit: high** with Graham and Feynman.

**Why:** Ambition is examined through observed research behavior and questions about choosing problems. **Transfer:** Make a reader confront a neglected choice, then explain the conditions that affect it. **Limit:** Preserve the distinction between remembered examples and systematic evidence. Use the argument, not a borrowed great-scientist persona.

### R02 · George Orwell · Politics and the English Language

[Estate-authorized text](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/). Suggested. Essay text sampled. **Fit: high** with the user's plain-language rules and Carlin's interest in language.

**Why:** Criticism is attached to actual passages and failures of meaning. **Transfer:** Ask which actor, action, or consequence a phrase conceals. **Limit:** Clarity rules serve meaning; do not mechanically simplify exact technical terms or rewrite an accurate quotation.

### R03 · Joan Didion · On Self-Respect

[Vogue's republication](https://www.vogue.com/article/joan-didion-self-respect-essay-1961). Suggested. Essay first published in 1961; opening sampled. **Fit: moderate** with Wallace and private reflection.

**Why:** A specific disappointment exposes the narrator's own assumptions. **Transfer:** Let the observed detail do emotional work; avoid an appended moral. **Limit:** Do not import the literary persona or invent personal history to obtain its effect.

### R04 · Derek Sivers · Subtract

[Author's essay](https://sive.rs/subtract). Suggested. Short text inspected. **Fit: high** with Altman's compression and Newport's focus.

**Why:** A small numerical illustration makes a mental habit visible with very little setup. **Transfer:** Use the minimum example that changes understanding. **Limit:** Aphoristic compression can erase conditions; retain the condition that matters to the reader.

### R05 · Bret Victor · Learnable Programming

[Author's interactive essay](https://worrydream.com/LearnableProgramming/). Suggested. September 2012. Text and descriptions sampled; interactive behaviors were not exhaustively tested. **Fit: high** with Feynman, Jobs, and Airey.

**Why:** Evaluates a system through what a learner can perceive and understand. **Transfer:** Make a design claim demonstrable through the user's task. **Limit:** The essay's argumentative sharpness is optional; the demonstration must still work.

### R06 · Ben Thompson · Aggregation Theory

[Original Stratechery essay](https://stratechery.com/2015/aggregation-theory/). Suggested. July 21, 2015. Text sampled. **Fit: high** with a16z analysis and Klein's mechanisms.

**Why:** A named model connects several cases by tracing relationships and changes in bargaining position. **Transfer:** State the mechanism before applying the label to a new market. **Limit:** A memorable theory is not evidence that every case fits it.

### R07 · Dan Luu · What to learn

[Author's essay](https://danluu.com/learn-what/). Suggested. Text sampled. **Fit: high** with engineering scrutiny and Graham's curiosity.

**Why:** Tests familiar advice by asking whether its rationale uniquely supports the recommendation. **Transfer:** Challenge a plausible explanation with alternatives and specific cases. **Limit:** Keep qualifications that affect the judgment; do not reproduce every digression in a short piece.

### R08 · Julia Evans · Teaching by filling in knowledge gaps

[Author's article](https://jvns.ca/blog/2021/09/20/teaching-by-filling-in-knowledge-gaps/). Suggested. September 20, 2021. Text sampled. **Fit: high** with Feynman and d.school practice.

**Why:** Starts with what someone can already do and explains the missing concept when it becomes useful. **Transfer:** Teach the exact gap blocking the reader, using a concrete failure or task. **Limit:** Match Shiv's audience; do not add beginner exposition to an expert design discussion.

### R09 · Atul Gawande · The Checklist

[Original New Yorker article](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2007/12/10/the-checklist). Suggested. Published online December 3, 2007; December 10 issue. Opening narrative sampled. **Fit: high** with Feynman's explanation and institutional analysis.

**Why:** Makes a system's complexity visible through a concrete case. **Transfer:** Establish the work and its failure modes before proposing process. **Limit:** The opening alone does not verify every later empirical claim; recheck original research for substantive use.

### R10 · Oliver Burkeman · The three-or-four-hours rule for getting creative work done

[Author's essay](https://www.oliverburkeman.com/fourhours). Suggested. Text sampled. **Fit: moderate** with Newport and reflection.

**Why:** Examines a practical limit while making room for objections and a light touch. **Transfer:** Explain what a constraint changes in a real day. **Limit:** Do not turn the named hour range into a universal prescription or confuse anecdotes with measurement.

### R11 · Patrick Collison · Fast

[Author's collection](https://patrickcollison.com/fast). Suggested. Entries sampled. **Fit: high** with Jobs, Graham, and d.school prototyping.

**Why:** A curated set of concrete cases challenges assumed timescales. **Transfer:** Use a compact evidence collection when cases are more useful than another abstract argument. **Limit:** Selected successes do not establish base rates; verify a case's dates and scope before reusing it.

## Stanford d.school

Shiv explicitly added the d.school's writing and design books. That establishes collection-level interest, not that every book below has been read or endorsed. The [official shop](https://dschool.stanford.edu/innovate/shop) lists the twelve-book collection, plus Make Space and other resources. D01–D13 below have official descriptions inspected; their full books were not read. Proposed applications are hypotheses based on those descriptions, not findings from stylistic measurement. D14 is a directly inspected writing resource.

Prioritize D01, D02, D03, D04, D05, and D14 for this writing skill. Keep the rest as a design shelf to draw on when the surface calls for it. Do not inherit promotional language from catalog copy.

### D01 · Charlotte Burgess-Auburn · You Need a Manifesto

[Official book page](https://dschool.stanford.edu/shop/you-need-a-manifesto). Collection. Subtitle: How to Craft Your Convictions and Put Them to Work.

**Study focus:** Gather and curate influences into a statement that guides actual choices. **Proposed application:** Make each voice principle change an editorial decision. **Boundary:** Admired language is input for synthesis, not text to paste into a new persona.

### D02 · Scott Witthoft · This Is a Prototype

[Official book page](https://dschool.stanford.edu/shop/this-is-a-prototype). Collection. Subtitle: The Curious Craft of Exploring New Ideas.

**Study focus:** Make something to reveal what is missing and learn what to do next. **Proposed application:** Test a paragraph, outline, or product explanation against a reader task before polishing the whole artifact. **Boundary:** An attractive draft is not evidence that the reader understands it.

### D03 · Leticia Britos Cavagnaro · Experiments in Reflection

[Official book page](https://dschool.stanford.edu/shop/experiments-in-reflection). Collection. Subtitle: How to See the Present, Reconsider the Past, and Shape the Future.

**Study focus:** The official description specifies experiments with a hypothesis, method, and way to assess results. **Proposed application:** Compare revisions against a stated reader need and record what changed. **Boundary:** Private reflection can remain unresolved; do not turn every journal entry into an experiment report.

### D04 · Carissa Carter · The Secret Language of Maps

[Official book page](https://dschool.stanford.edu/shop/the-secret-language-of-maps). Collection. Subtitle: How to Tell Visual Stories with Data.

**Study focus:** Examine how selection and presentation shape what a map or infographic communicates; the book uses a mystery narrative. **Proposed application:** Explain what a visual includes, omits, and makes easy to compare. **Boundary:** A diagram must carry a real relationship, not decorate the prose.

### D05 · Sarah Stein Greenberg · Creative Acts for Curious People

[Official book page](https://dschool.stanford.edu/shop/creative-acts-for-curious-people). Collection. Subtitle: How to Think, Create, and Lead in Unconventional Ways.

**Study focus:** Practice observation and creativity through assignments, stories, and concrete activity. **Proposed application:** Give teaching pieces an exercise that reveals whether the concept transferred. **Boundary:** Keep activities proportional to the task; a routine explanation does not need a workshop.

### D06 · Andrea Small and Kelly Schmutte · Navigating Ambiguity

[Official book page](https://dschool.stanford.edu/shop/navigating-ambiguity). Collection. Subtitle: Creating Opportunity in a World of Unknowns.

**Study focus:** Work with uncertainty through curiosity and adaptation. **Proposed application:** Distinguish an open question from an unsupported claim; identify the next observation that would help. **Boundary:** Neither false certainty nor endless qualification makes a decision better.

### D07 · Ashish Goel · Drawing on Courage

[Official book page](https://dschool.stanford.edu/shop/drawing-on-courage). Collection. Subtitle: Risks Worth Taking and Stands Worth Making.

**Study focus:** Connect values, fear, and action through approachable design tools. **Proposed application:** Express a defensible position and the risk involved in acting on it. **Boundary:** Courage does not require chest-thumping or unsupported certainty.

### D08 · Susie Wise · Design for Belonging

[Official book page](https://dschool.stanford.edu/shop/design-for-belonging). Collection. Subtitle: How to Build Inclusion and Collaboration in Your Communities.

**Study focus:** How roles, spaces, and shared events shape participation. **Proposed application:** Make product and instructional writing intelligible to its actual participants. **Boundary:** State the concrete friction or design decision; do not substitute values language for an explanation.

### D09 · Olatunde Sobomehin and sam seidel · Creative Hustle

[Official book page](https://dschool.stanford.edu/shop/creative-hustle). Collection. Subtitle: Blaze Your Own Path and Make Work That Matters. The lowercase styling of sam seidel's name follows the official page.

**Study focus:** Connect a person's particular capabilities with a path they can act on. **Proposed application:** Ground a career or founder narrative in actual choices and constraints. **Boundary:** Do not make aspiration stand in for demonstrated experience.

### D10 · Lesley-Ann Noel · Design Social Change

[Official book page](https://dschool.stanford.edu/shop/design-social-change). Collection. Subtitle: Take Action, Work toward Equity, and Challenge the Status Quo.

**Study focus:** Relate the designer's position and motivations to the people affected by a change. **Proposed application:** In social or institutional analysis, identify whose perspective the proposed solution assumes. **Boundary:** Do not assign Shiv beliefs merely because this book belongs to a liked collection.

### D11 · Grace Hawthorne · Make Possibilities Happen

[Official book page](https://dschool.stanford.edu/shop/make-possibilities-happen). Collection. Subtitle: How to Transform Ideas into Reality.

**Study focus:** Turn an idea into a concrete attempt and learn through beginning and continuing. **Proposed application:** End practical guidance with an executable first move when one is needed. **Boundary:** Remove motivational copy that does not help someone act.

### D12 · Scott Doorley and Carissa Carter · Assembling Tomorrow

[Official book page](https://dschool.stanford.edu/shop/assembling-tomorrow). Collection. Subtitle: A Guide to Designing a Thriving Future.

**Study focus:** The described form combines design concepts, practical actions, and speculative stories. **Proposed application:** Use clearly labeled scenarios to explore consequences in strategy writing. **Boundary:** Keep fiction, forecast, and current evidence visibly distinct.

### D13 · Scott Doorley and Scott Witthoft · Make Space

[Official book page](https://dschool.stanford.edu/shop/make-space). Collection-adjacent title on the official shop. Subtitle: How to Set the Stage for Creative Collaboration.

**Study focus:** Connect environments and concrete arrangements to collaboration. **Proposed application:** Explain how layout, proximity, or an interface changes behavior. **Boundary:** Visual taste alone does not establish a causal effect.

### D14 · Stanford d.school contributors · How to Write: Rituals and Reminders from the Stanford d.school

[Official article](https://dschool.stanford.edu/stories/how-to-write-rituals-and-reminders-from-the-stanford-d-school). Collection-related writing resource. February 6, 2025. Text sampled.

**Craft:** Writers describe concrete drafting practices, including collecting ideas, tolerating poor beginnings, and making a structure visible. **Transfer:** Separate gathering, drafting, and revision; use a small inspectable artifact to move the work forward. **Limit:** These are individual practices, not evidence that a particular ritual will work for every writer.

## Evidence boundaries

The corpus supports a provisional set of craft moves. It does not support numerical blend weights, a target sentence-length distribution, claims that all works reflect Shiv's beliefs, or promises about AI-detector outcomes. User-accepted edits should increasingly determine the writing skill; additions to the reading shelf should not automatically become mandatory instructions.
