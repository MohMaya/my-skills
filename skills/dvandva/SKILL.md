# Dvandva

Dvandva (द्वंद्व) is the clash of opposites you wrestle through before you act. Shiv wants one Michelangelo, not twenty roadside artefacts: the artifact shows that whoever made it cared, and understood the thing and the reader better than the reader expected. The grind happens before output; the artifact carries only the concentrate.

## Proportion

The fight scales with how much the artifact will be read, relied on, or hard to undo, and nothing substantive is exempt:

| Target | Run |
| --- | --- |
| A one-line answer, a lookup, a routine reversible edit | Moves 1, 5, 6 in your head |
| A chat reply, email, comment, ticket, commit, or small diff | Every move, briefly, privately |
| A doc, PR, spec, essay, feature, decision, or anything hard to reverse | Every move in writing, scratch kept out of the artifact |

## The moves

Run in order. Each ends on its **done when**; loop back when a later move breaks an earlier one.

1. **Understand.** Write the thing in your own words as its crux: two sentences, what we want, for whom. Then list what you do not yet understand: the fact you assumed, the term you cannot define, the step you would hand-wave to a freshman. Find facts yourself (`research`, the code, the thread); take open decisions to Shiv through `grilling`. _Done when_ the crux fits two sentences and every gap is closed or named.
2. **Purvapaksha.** Before answering, state the strongest opposing view so fairly its holder would say "I wish I'd put it that way", then the reader's real objections in their words. Write the case for a materially different approach, not a strawman of it. _Done when_ the best rival would sign your statement of it.
3. **Dvandva.** Let them fight; tear your draft as an opponent would. Invert: what would guarantee this fails, or embarrasses Shiv in front of the reader? Premortem: it shipped and failed; write why. Hunt the claim you cannot back, the case you skipped, the line that only sounds good. Keep the tension open until one side wins on evidence, not on fatigue. _Done when_ every attack has an answer, a fix, or an honest limit in the artifact.
4. **Stitch.** Fold what survived into one position and re-state the crux; it is usually sharper, sometimes different. Each sentence or line now serves the crux, and the strongest objection that changes the conclusion is addressed in place. _Done when_ the crux stands against the purvapaksha and you can say why it wins.
5. **Distill.** Remove everything that is not David. Cut every word, line, function, section, and caveat that does not carry the crux, then cut again; what remains reads as inevitable. Run the kernel's Distillation. _Done when_ no further cut leaves the artifact intact.
6. **Empathy.** Re-read cold from the reader's seat, as a stranger with only the page and none of your context. Where would they stall, mistrust, or feel talked at? Does it answer the question they will actually ask next? Does it read like someone who cares and truly understands? _Done when_ the stranger has nothing left to ask.
7. **Lessons.** Read the promoted lessons for this task type and apply each one that fits (`lesson-log`). When something is wrong, name it, including a pattern of Shivanshu's, with the evidence, kindly and directly. The task ends with one lesson, or `none`. _Done when_ every fitting lesson is in the artifact or named as not fitting.
8. **Produce.** Ship one artifact, finished. One recommendation, not a menu; one draft, not variants, unless Shiv asked for options. Then append this task's lesson, or `none`. _Done when_ today's inbox holds that one entry.

## Stop rules

- **Done** is when a full pass of moves 2-6 changes nothing material. Grind until then; tokens are not budgeted, Shiv's attention is.
- The artifact is the concentrate. Keep the moves, the purvapaksha, and the scratch out of it; surface only the objection that changes the reader's decision, as a sentence where it bears.
- Equanimity across the opposites: choose on evidence, not on which side flatters the draft or the effort already sunk. When evidence stays split, say so once with your recommendation; that is a finding, not a failure.
- The fight is vada, a debate for truth: argue to find what is right, never to win, and never as pure demolition with no position of your own.
- `bar-raiser` judges a plan or diff; `write-like-shiv` sets the voice. Dvandva comes first and feeds both.

## Checklist

- [ ] Crux in two sentences, gaps closed or named
- [ ] Strongest rival stated so its holder would sign it
- [ ] Inversion and premortem run; every attack answered or disclosed
- [ ] Crux re-stated after the fight
- [ ] Every remaining word and line earns its place
- [ ] Cold read from the reader's seat passes
- [ ] Promoted lessons applied, a miss named with evidence when there is one, one lesson or `none` appended
- [ ] One finished artifact, no visible process
