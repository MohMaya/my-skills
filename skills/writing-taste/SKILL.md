# Writing taste

A library of moves studied from the highest-signal writers, so prose earns its conviction. `write-like-shiv` owns the voice; this skill supplies the thinking and craft that voice applies.

## Use it

1. Pick the genre of the piece you are writing and read one or two matching studies in [`references/studies/`](references/studies/). Each ends with an exercise.
2. Think before writing: draft the core claim in one sentence. If you cannot, you do not understand the subject yet; write to find out, then rewrite.
3. Draft, then run the checklist below and fix every miss.

## Principles

Promoted only after a pattern appears in two or more studies, each naming its sources. None yet; candidates wait below until a second study confirms them.

## Candidate moves (one study each)

- **Writing is the test of the idea.** Write to find out what you think; expect half the ideas to arrive while writing. ([Graham, words](references/studies/2026-10-11-putting-ideas-into-words.md))
- **Pass the neutral stranger.** Reread as a reader who knows only the page; add what they need even if it costs nice sentences. (Graham, words)
- **Escalate from the obvious.** Establish a premise readers already accept, then follow it one step further to the claim that surprises. (Graham, words)
- **Concede with a boundary.** Answer the strongest counterexample by redrawing exactly where the claim holds. (Graham, words)
- **End calibrated.** Close by sizing the claim, necessary rather than sufficient, instead of inflating it. (Graham, words)

## Checklist before sending

- The core claim fits in one sentence, and the opening states or sets it up.
- A neutral stranger would find it correct and complete.
- The strongest objection is answered, with the boundary of the claim stated.
- Every abstraction has one concrete example or image.
- Each paragraph moves the argument; asides live in notes or are cut.
- The ending is as strong as the evidence and no stronger.
