# How to be more ambitious — Felix Rieseberg

- Author: Felix Rieseberg (@felixrieseberg), October 13, 2026
- Genre: tutorial essay (product writing that teaches a method)
- Found via: Shiv named this piece for the Oct 15, 2026 study
- Studied: 2026-10-15

## Core claim

Ambition with a model is three climbs on the task you already have—altitude (the outcome above the task), depth (the expert's instruments on the raw material), and range (another field's method)—while speed, the same work in less time, is granted and left out, and the difficulty is self-efficacy, an old limit now more powerful because capability moves every few weeks.

## Thinking moves

- **Split the slogan, then skip the settled kind.** "Be more ambitious" is already a rallying cry, too vague to follow. He breaks it into Speed, Altitude, Depth, and Range, grants Speed as "the bread and butter for AI's economic value," and refuses to teach it. The lesson starts at the gap.
- **Hold one ordinary task and change one axis.** A quarterly deck at an imagined Acme Inc is deliberately mundane. Altitude rewrites what the deck is for. Depth changes the instruments used on that same deck. Range changes which fields touch it. The reader tracks the idea because the setup never moves.
- **Demand the instrument, not the costume.** Asking the model to "act like a professional" changes vocabulary and leaves the method. He wants the raw material, the tool a leading practitioner would actually open, and a judge who is not the maker. A short seller is hired for the research a short seller does, not for a voice.
- **Put the boundary in the same breath as the technique.** Altitude "works less well for creative endeavors." He dates the open failure: ("Claude, go make money" doesn't seem to work for most businesses by itself in 2026). Roleplay "will make your transcript weirder without improving the actual outcome." Each limit is dated and local, so the climbs stay usable.
- **Teach the moves, then name the old constraint.** The tutorial is finished before "We have been here before." Self-efficacy, Locke and Latham, Dantzig's homework, Bannister's 46 days, Linux as "just a hobby." The close sizes it: an old problem, more powerful now, and he asks for techniques he missed.

## Writing moves

- **Opening:** two beats of scene (feats people are already pulling off; the slogan everyone reposts), then the four kinds, before any tactic.
- **Structure:** taxonomy, skip Speed, one office task climbed on altitude then depth then range, one section compressed into a four-part procedure, then the prior name for why the advice was hard. How, then why.
- **Transitions:** the headings are the kinds. Inside the first climb: "We'll start basic and get a little bit more ambitious with each step." Before the psychology: "That's my concrete advice for how to be more ambitious."
- **Rhythm:** a one-sentence definition, then the ask in a sentence the reader could say tomorrow. The skip lands as its own clause at the end of the Speed bullet.
- **Concrete examples:** crash reports kept crash-free, once the model can fetch them and reproduce the bug; the deck replaced by a check that would have caught last quarter sooner; markdown tables refused for a database; a ten-second page sent back for 200 milliseconds.
- **What he cut:** no benchmark tour, no Speed lesson, no product manual. A computer plus the relevant data is one paragraph. The psychology is three incidents, each one story.
- **Ending:** the claim stops at "more powerful," not at a new theory of ambition. The last sentence asks the reader for techniques he missed.

## Excerpts

1. The cut: "Well established and requires little creativity in how you approach AI, so I'll skip this one."
2. The climb, as a sentence the reader can say: "The review exists so leadership catches problems early and moves money and people to the right places. Design a better way to do that than a quarterly deck, show from our own history that it would have caught things sooner..."
3. Costume versus method: "When prompted to act like a professional, models often switch vocabulary without actually switching methods."
4. Instrument, not costume: "Don't ask the model to write a short seller, ask it to do what a short seller does to research their position."
5. Sized close: "Self-efficacy isn't a new problem, but it is a more powerful one now."

## Exercise

Pick a slogan you already tell people in your field. Split it into three or four kinds. Name the kind they already do, and leave it out of the lesson. Choose one ordinary task from last week and rewrite the ask three times on that same task: the outcome above the task, the raw material and instrument a leading practitioner would use, and one method from a field the task does not usually hire. Name the check that is not you. Close with the old constraint this is an instance of, and the case where the climb stops.
