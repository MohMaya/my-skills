## ECB economists Barauskaitė Griškevičienė & Brand, "Why the drivers of inflation matter for monetary policy" (ecb.europa.eu blog)

Source/Author: ECB staff blog, 1 Sept 2026 → the post carries a contested
policy argument entirely on attribution: "adverse energy supply factors
accounted for around 90% of the increase" — the percentage does the arguing,
never the adverb. The piece names its model (BVAR, 2007–2026) in one clause and
moves on, so rigor reads as habitat rather than display. → Apply when defending
a number-heavy claim: one attributed statistic per sentence, model named once,
no "remarkably." → Do not apply in scene-first storytelling, where the figure
arrives late and earns its place.

## Acquired, "Disney: The Renaissance and the Empire" — chapter titles as a timeline

Source/Author: Acquired episode notes (Apple Podcasts) → every chapter title
pairs a state of affairs with a year: "Disney in Chaos (1984)", "Eisner's
Decline & Save Disney Campaign (2001-2004)". The titles alone compress four

where dated headers are overhead.

## Paul Graham, "How to Do Great Work" — the oracle device

Source/Author: Paul Graham → to fix his central word without shouting it, he
invents a concrete hypothetical that re-issues the claim as a wager: "If you
asked an oracle the secret to doing great work and the oracle replied with a
single word, my bet would be on 'curiosity.'" The repetition arrives dressed as
new information, so emphasis never reads as emphasis. → Apply once, near the
close of an argument whose key term has appeared several times and risks
going stale. → Never more than once per piece, and never with real sources —
the oracle must be visibly invented.

## Tversky & Kahneman, Science 1974 — paired cells as an argument

Source/Author: Tversky & Kahneman, "Judgment under Uncertainty" (Science, 1974)
→ the anchoring claim rests on paired experimental cells: identical question,
rigged wheel at 10 vs 65, medians 25 vs 45. No verdict sentence is needed; the
difference between two numbers is the whole argument, and the paper adds one
clause — "a number known to be meaningless" — to seal it. → Apply when a piece
hinges on a causal claim: present the control and the variant side by side and
let the delta speak; keep commentary to a single clause. → Do not apply where
the evidence is narrative rather than comparative; two anecdotes side by side
is not an experiment.

## Constituent Assembly Debates, Vol. XI — the minutes as a narrative spine

Source/Author: Constituent Assembly of India, Debates Vol. XI (26 Nov 1949) →
the sitting's own sequence carries the drama: Patel's states announcement
first, interruptions about Vande Mataram and a Hindi text in the middle, then
Prasad's cost ledger (Rs 63,96,729, 53,000 visitors) as a report card. A

record is long and flat; select one sitting, never summarize a whole volume.

## Own draft, Firm — Demand slot (Qwikster outline) — the one-bullet distinction

Source/Author: my companion-article outline, Sept 3 → the outline's strongest
move is a two-line separation: "What the price did" versus "What the envelope
did," letting an author own one change and disown the other. → Apply when a
piece must make a narrow claim inside a familiar case history: attribute to the

cause; the distinction then reads as hedging.
